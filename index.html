KinoGen: Customizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation References via Kinematically Grounded Video Generation

Qi Wu1, Lulu Zhao1, Sanghyun Hahn1, Rishi Yennu1, Emmy Yang1, Dingqi Zhang2, Tao Pang, Jiuguang Wang2, Kuan Fang1
1Cornell University 2

Abstract

We present KinoGen, a framework that synthesizes customizable humanoid loco-manipulation references through kinematically grounded video generation. KinoGen conditions a pretrained video generator on calibrated renders of the target robot and object, then fits the same models to the generated two-view video. The resulting reference lies in the robot configuration space without a human body proxy or retargeting. By correcting a single keyframe, KinoGen prescribes a goal object pose, regenerates the surrounding motion, and recovers a reference that satisfies that pose exactly. Across eight tasks, KinoGen reduces penetration and foot skating, preserves over 90% of annotated contacts, and supports tracking-policy deployment on a Unitree G1 with dexterous hands.

Method

Tracking policies for humanoid loco-manipulation require motion references that specify whole-body, hand, and object trajectories in the configuration space of the target robot. Video generation offers a scalable source of such behavior. However, generated videos provide pixels rather than trajectories, and they give no guarantee that a prescribed object pose is reached.

Our key insight is that a video generator and a physics simulator can be coupled through rendering and reconstruction when both operate on the same robot and object models. This coupling leads to three design choices:

  • Conditioning on the rendered initial state. Calibrated renders of the initial robot and object state condition the video generator, which places the generated behavior in the exact embodiment and scene. Fitting the same models to the generated video returns the behavior to the simulator. This yields references in the robot configuration space without a human body proxy or retargeting.
  • Multi-view reconstruction with motion priors. Two synchronized views of the same interaction reduce depth ambiguity. Whole-body, hand, and object priors constrain the motion that image evidence alone cannot resolve.
  • Keyframe-based correction. A corrected simulator keyframe prescribes the goal. The generator regenerates the surrounding motion, and reconstruction enforces the keyframe as a hard constraint. The reference reaches the prescribed configuration exactly while the motion stays coherent.
